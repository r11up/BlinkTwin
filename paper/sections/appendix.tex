\section{Workload model}
\label{app:workload}
BlinkTwin also keeps an estimate of perceived workload $w(t)$ on the raw NASA-TLX scale (0--100)~\cite{hart1988development,hart2006nasa}. NASA-TLX rates a completed task, so treating a rating as a point observation of a momentary state is an approximation; the check-in therefore asks about the work block just finished. Workload reverts towards the demand $\mu_c$ of the current context $c$ as an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process with time constant $\tau_w = 20$~min and stationary SD $\sigma_\infty = 18$. Over a step of $\Delta$ hours, with $\beta = e^{-60\Delta/\tau_w}$,
\begin{equation}
  w \leftarrow \mu_c + (w - \mu_c)\,\beta, \qquad P_w \leftarrow P_w\,\beta^2 + \sigma_\infty^2\,(1 - \beta^2).
\end{equation}
The demand of each context starts from a default $D_c$ (break 15, reading 40, focused work 65, meeting 50, other or unset 40) and is shrunk towards the person's own TLX ratings in that context, $\mu_c = (2D_c + \sum_i y_{c,i})/(2 + n_c)$. A TLX rating updates $w$ with noise SD $\sigma_T = 8$; the initial state is 40 with SD 20. The webcam is not used for workload. The defaults are assumptions, the same rating enters both the learned demand and the update, and the component has not been evaluated; it is therefore described as a feature of the application, not as a validated estimate of cognitive load~\cite{paas2003cognitive}.

\section{Three-Process Model constants}
\label{app:tpm}
Table~\ref{tab:apptpm} lists every constant of the prior with its source. Each value was checked against \cite{akerstedt2004predictions} and \cite{ingre2014validating}. The linear recovery rate below the breakpoint is the exponential's slope at the breakpoint, $\delta_s(H - B) = 0.80$ units~h$^{-1}$, following the construction in \cite{akerstedt2004predictions}. Sleep inertia uses the values of \cite{ingre2014validating}; the 2004 paper lists $W_{\mathrm{Decay}} = 0.65692$, which equals 1.51 divided by $\ln 10$ and is therefore probably a base-10 rate. Ingre et al.\ found that adding $W$ worsened the fit to airline data and refitted the KSS mapping to $a_K = 9.68$, $b_K = 0.46$~\cite{ingre2014validating}; Section~\ref{sec:openneuro} tests that mapping on OpenNeuro ds004902.

\section{DROZY: descriptive results}
\label{app:drozy}
Table~\ref{tab:appdrozy} gives the means of every measure by test, for the landmark pipeline, the website's MediaPipe pipeline and the EOG; only aggregates are shown, following the dataset's licence. Fig.~\ref{fig:appblink} shows the agreement of the landmark blink rates with EOG. In random-intercept mixed models, KSS rose by 2.22 points per 100~ms of median reaction time (95\% CI 1.07 to 3.37), 0.28 points per percentage point of PERCLOS (0.08 to 0.48) and 1.92 points per 100~ms of blink duration (0.71 to 3.14); these Wald intervals are anti-conservative with 14 clusters, the models do not separate within- from between-person effects, and the blink-rate model did not converge. They are reported for comparison with the earlier draft; the released model uses the within-person forward models of the main text instead. Per-subject slopes of KSS on reaction time, for the 9 subjects with three tests, ranged from 2.5 to 7.5 points per 100~ms, all positive; with three points per subject their spread cannot be separated from estimation noise.

\paragraph*{Replay details} In each fold of the leave-one-subject-out replay (Section~\ref{sec:replay}, \texttt{web/scripts/drozy\_twin.ts}), three numbers per signal were refitted on the other 13 subjects with the within-person regression of the main text: the slope $b$, the population level $a$ (the mean of the subjects' intercepts) and the between-person SD of the anchor (the spread of the intercepts less its share of measurement noise). Everything else kept its released value: the measurement-noise SDs and their inflation for serial correlation, the slope spread, the anchor drift, the offset's prior and drift, the time-on-task term and all Three-Process Model constants. Times were the local clock times in the PVT files, used without a time-zone shift. The test's PVT result entered 10~min after the test started, at the end of the 10-min test; each minute of eye features entered at the end of its minute, and blink duration only from minutes with at least three blinks. DROZY records no time for the KSS rating, so each rating was placed 11~min after the test started, and each prediction was made just before it, from all earlier events: the same test's PVT and eye features were therefore used, its rating was not. The landmark features carry no blink-peak value, so the PERCLOS guard of Section~\ref{sec:system} did not apply and PERCLOS was always used.

\section{OpenNeuro ds004902: details}
\label{app:openneuro}
Fig.~\ref{fig:appopenneuro} shows the KSS and PVT median reaction time in the two sessions and the coupling of the reaction-time change with the SSS change. For the offset analysis, the KSS time was the eyes-open EEG time of each session; the normal-sleep session assumed a 7.5-h night ending at 07:00 and the deprivation session assumed waking at 07:00 on the previous day. The sleep-diary analysis used the diary's bedtime and get-up time for the normal-sleep night (28 people with KSS in both sessions and a diary between 3 and 13~h), and the diary's get-up time as the previous wake time for the deprivation session. The shrinkage factor for one check-in was the Kalman gain $\sigma_d^2/(\sigma_d^2 + \sigma_K^2) = 0.72$; in the persistence analysis it was estimated from the data as 0.77.

\section{Simulation details}
\label{app:sim}
\paragraph*{Virtual people} Person-level parameters: offset $o \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.84)$; circadian phase shift $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$~h; time-on-task effect $\mathcal{N}(0.4, 0.2)$ KSS~h$^{-1}$, truncated at 0; slow fluctuation of sleepiness (Ornstein--Uhlenbeck, SD 0.5, time constant 1.5~h); rating noise SD 0.7, rounded and clipped to 1--9. Each passive feature follows $a_i + b_i (s^* - 3)$ with $a_i \sim \mathcal{N}(a, \sigma_\alpha)$ and $b_i \sim \mathcal{N}(b, \cdot)$ truncated at 0, using the DROZY means of Table~\ref{tab:params} but smaller slope spreads (SD 8~ms, 7~ms and 0.3\% for reaction time, blink duration and PERCLOS) than the filter assumes (7.9~ms, 14.3~ms and 0.47\%). The filter's values are the raw spread of per-person slopes from the 8 subjects with three usable sessions, an upper bound that includes estimation noise; the simulator's values are a judgement of the true spread. The matched scenario therefore does not test a filter that underestimates slope heterogeneity. Webcam baselines drift (Ornstein--Uhlenbeck, SD 30~ms for blink duration and 1.8\% for PERCLOS, time constant 4~h) and per-minute noise is AR(1) with $\rho = 0.02$ (blink duration) and 0.32 (PERCLOS). Truncations: median reaction time $\geq 180$~ms; blink duration $\geq 60$~ms; PERCLOS in 0--60\%; blink rate $\mathcal{N}(18, 6)$ per minute, rounded, with blink duration missing below 3 blinks; face coverage fixed at 0.9.

\paragraph*{Scenarios} \emph{Misreport}: circadian phase SD 2~h and actual sleep differing from the reported 7.5~h with SD 1.5~h. \emph{Nonlinear}: signals respond with 0.6 of the slope up to KSS~6 and an added quadratic term above it; Student-$t_3$ noise. \emph{Rating bias}: a personal bias SD 0.8 added to every rating and rating noise SD 1.0. \emph{Autocorrelated webcam}: per-minute noise AR(1) with $\rho = 0.7$ and larger baseline drift. \emph{Trait}: homeostatic decay multiplied by $e^{\mathcal{N}(0, 0.3)}$ and circadian amplitude by $e^{\mathcal{N}(0, 0.2)}$ per person. \emph{Debt}: five previous nights of 5~h, of which the model is told only the last. \emph{Workday}: 16~h awake (07:30--23:00), check-ins 08:30--21:30, forecasts 09:00--22:00. \emph{Dissociation}: ratings equal the truth up to KSS~5 and include only half of any further rise.
